🛠️ Root Causes Fixed:
Object Allocation & Disposal Flood in PeashooterWeapon.js:

Previously, every trail particle (40 per second per bullet), muzzle flash, and impact splat was instantiating new THREE.SphereGeometry, THREE.DodecahedronGeometry, and THREE.MeshBasicMaterial objects and disposing them immediately on death.
Instantiating and disposing 100+ WebGL geometries and materials per second caused GPU buffer re-allocations and severe Garbage Collection (GC) frame freezes.
Fix: Pre-allocated shared geometries (particleGeom, trailGeom, projGeoms) and shared materials in PeashooterWeapon.js. Reused existing geometry/material instances across all active projectiles and particles with strict caps (MAX_PARTICLES = 50, MAX_PROJECTILES = 15).
Heavy Recursive Raycasting on 50+ Trees:

triggerAction() in main.js was running recursive intersectObjects across all 50+ tree groups and leaf meshes on every shot.




1. Vector and Matrix Allocations in Update Loops
The Problem: Instantiating objects like new THREE.Vector3(), new THREE.Quaternion(), or new THREE.Matrix4() inside your animation loop or weapon update tick creates thousands of temporary objects per second, even if geometries are pooled.

2. Texture and Render Target VRAM Leaks
The Problem: While your geometries and materials are now shared, dynamically loaded textures, video textures, or WebGLRenderTarget instances often bypass standard garbage collection. If you switch levels, reload weapons, or create UI/particle textures dynamically without calling .dispose(), VRAM usage will continuously climb until the browser crashes.

The Fix: Explicitly call .dispose() on textures, render targets, and custom framebuffers when cleaning up or destroying components.

3. Scene Graph and Event Reference Leaks
The Problem: Even if you remove a mesh from the scene, if an array (like an active projectile list) or an event listener still holds a reference to it, JavaScript's Garbage Collector cannot clear it from memory.
The Fix: Ensure that when projectiles or particles die, they are not only hidden or pooled, but completely detached from parent groups (mesh.removeFromParent()) and cleared from any tracking arrays or event subscriptions.
